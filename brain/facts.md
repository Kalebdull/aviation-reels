# Verified facts

Every claim a reel makes lives here with its source. Check here before researching; add to it after. If a fact has no source yet, it's marked **NEEDS SOURCE** and can't go in a script until it's verified.

Sources to prefer: FAA *Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge* (PHAK, FAA-H-8083-25), *Instrument Flying Handbook* (IFH, FAA-H-8083-15), *Airplane Flying Handbook* (AFH, FAA-H-8083-3), *Aeronautical Information Manual* (AIM), the ACS, and 14 CFR. Cite the chapter or section.

Format: `- **Claim.** Detail. — Source`

## Spatial disorientation / IMC
- **Non-instrument pilots lost control in simulated IMC in an average of 178 seconds.** 20 VFR pilots, 1954 University of Illinois study ("178 seconds to live"). — Bryan, Stonecipher & Aron, *180-Degree Turn Experiment*, Univ. of Illinois Aeronautical Bulletin 54 (1954); summarised by AOPA. ## Pitot-static system
- **The pitot tube feeds ram air to the airspeed indicator only.** — PHAK ch. 8 (Flight Instruments), Pitot-Static Flight Instruments
- **The static port feeds the ASI, the altimeter and the VSI.** — PHAK ch. 8
- **Pressure drops about 1 inHg per 1,000 ft in the lower atmosphere.** — PHAK ch. 4 / ch. 8
- **The VSI compares the pressure in the diaphragm (instant) with the case (fed through a calibrated leak).** — PHAK ch. 8, Vertical Speed Indicator
- **A blocked pitot tube with its drain hole still open makes the airspeed drop toward zero.** With the drain hole blocked too, the ASI acts like an altimeter (it reads higher as you climb). — PHAK ch. 8, Blockage of the Pitot-Static System

## VOR
- **A VOR transmits 360 radials, referenced to magnetic north.** — PHAK ch. 16 (Navigation), VOR; AIM 1-1-3
- **A reference signal is the same in every direction while a variable signal rotates; the phase difference between them is the radial.** — PHAK ch. 16
- **The TO/FROM flag depends on the selected course, not the aircraft's heading.** — PHAK ch. 16; IFH ch. 9
- **Victor airways connect VORs.** — AIM 5-3-4; PHAK ch. 16

## Approaches
- **A precision approach can go down to a decision altitude 200 ft above the touchdown zone (Cat I ILS).** — AIM 5-4-5; IFH ch. 10
- **Sequenced flashing lights ("the rabbit") run toward the threshold.** — AIM 2-1-1, Approach Light Systems

## GPS / RAIM / WAAS
- **GPS satellites orbit at about 10,900 nautical miles (about 12,550 statute miles).** Signal travel time is about 0.067 s. — PHAK ch. 16; AIM 1-1-17
- **There are about 30 satellites in the constellation (24 is the baseline).** — AIM 1-1-17
- **3 satellites give a 2D fix (if altitude is known); 4 give 3D.** — AIM 1-1-17
- **RAIM needs 5 satellites to detect a fault and 6 to exclude one (or 5 with baro-aiding).** — AIM 1-1-17
- **Without RAIM there's no integrity assurance, and an IFR receiver alerts the pilot.** — AIM 1-1-17
- **WAAS uses ground reference stations at surveyed locations (including Canada and Mexico); corrections go up to geostationary satellites and are broadcast down, along with integrity warnings.** — AIM 1-1-18
- **Satellites broadcast their position and the time the signal was sent.** — AIM 1-1-17. On screen, show coordinates and a clock, not "here I am".
