# Lessons learned (keep updating)

After each post, Kaleb shares the numbers (views, average watch time, retention at 3 s, saves, sends, follows) and any feedback. Add a dated line here with **what happened → what we change**, then fold anything permanent into `playbook.md` or `style.md`.

## Carried over from the earlier Instrument 101 series (TikTok)
- **Never open on a static title card.** Ep 9 did and 63.6% skipped. Frame 0 is already moving and the hook is spoken in the first second.
- **Fix the first 3 seconds before anything else.** Ep 8 (holding patterns) reached ~21K views and 104 follows; retention fell to ~45% in 6 s, then stayed flat.
- **Saves and shares beat likes.** Lean on checkride and quiz angles.
- **Part 2s ride the Part 1 wave.** Post follow-ups fast.
- **Every gauge or system needs a working demo, not just a dial.** The pitot-vs-static airspeed demo was loved; plain dials for the altimeter and VSI were rejected.
- **Payoff moments need a cinematic visual.** "Then the lights appear" needed the pilot's-eye windshield view: runway out of the fog, the rabbit racing to the threshold.
- **Long videos lose people.** A 104 s version lost viewers at ~16 s. The viral median in the niche was ~41 s. The format is 35–45 s (Kaleb, Nov 2026).
- **Voice/file mix-ups happen.** CapCut exports arrive with random names: always confirm which script a voice file belongs to (vo.py prints the detected lines) and check it isn't silent.

## Best performers so far (study these before every script)
- **Holding patterns, ~32 s, 62K views (best).** Why it worked:
  1. The hook is an everyday observation everyone has had: "Why do planes fly in circles?" A curiosity gap, zero jargon.
  2. It names the keyword immediately: "It's called a holding pattern." (search + clarity)
  3. Three quick reasons as three mini-scenes, about 1 s each: busy airport, bad weather, ATC "Please hold." Rapid variety.
  4. **One visual motif that builds**: the glowing racetrack appears, then gets a timer, then wind, then a stack. The viewer watches one picture grow.
  5. Real numbers on screen: Outbound 0:24, 5,000–8,000 ft.
  6. A community ending: "What should we break down next?" → comments.
  7. About 95 words in 32 s, roughly 3 words a second.
- **VOR TO/FROM flag (escalator).** A familiar physical analogy (facing ≠ going) carried the whole idea before any instrument appeared.
- **Direction from Kaleb (Nov 2026):** keep that clarity, but make it feel like a **cartoon show**: super flowing, good graphics everyone loves to watch. A living world the camera travels through, characters with a little personality, weather that moves, planes that bank into their turns.
