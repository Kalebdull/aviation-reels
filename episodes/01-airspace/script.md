# Ep 01: Airspace (for someone hearing about it for the first time)

Status: DRAFT v2 (waiting for Kaleb's approval)

## Direction from Kaleb
One simple bubble that shows what airspace looks like, plus one fact non-aviators can relate to. They've never heard of it, so we turn nothing into something with a relatable explanation. The opener must be something everyone relates to.

## Hook formula
**Familiar experience + curiosity gap:** "Every time you land at a big airport, you fly through an invisible bubble." Almost everyone has looked out the window on landing; we tell them something they never knew about that moment.

## One idea
Big airports sit inside an invisible bubble of airspace that you need permission to enter. It's huge, and it's shaped like an upside-down wedding cake.

## Script (paste into CapCut text-to-speech)
Every time you land at a big airport, you fly through an invisible bubble. . . . Pilots call it airspace. . . . Think of it like a gated neighborhood in the sky. . . . To get in, you need permission from air traffic control. . . . And this bubble is huge. . . . Around the busiest airports, it reaches 10,000 feet up. . . . That's more than six Empire State Buildings stacked on top of each other. . . . But it's not a dome. It's shaped like an upside-down wedding cake. . . . Small near the ground, wider as it climbs, so small planes can fly underneath the edges. . . . So next time you land, look out the window. You're inside the cake. . . . What should we break down next?

108 words, about 36–38 s.

## Sources
- Class B: generally surface to 10,000 ft MSL around the busiest airports; layered, "some Class B airspace areas resemble upside-down wedding cakes"; ATC clearance required. FAA PHAK ch. 15, Airspace.
- Empire State Building: 1,454 ft to the antenna tip (Wikipedia) or 1,250 ft to the roof (CTBUH). 10,000 ft is 6.9 or 8 buildings, so "more than six" is true either way.
- Flying under the shelves: the outer tiers start above the ground, so aircraft can operate beneath them without entering (PHAK ch. 15 layer description; Figure 15-1).

## Notes / questions for Kaleb
- "Big airport" means the busiest airports (Class B: e.g. LAX, Atlanta, Dallas). Medium airports have a smaller bubble (Class C). We don't name classes in this one, to keep it simple. Part 2 could be "the other bubbles".
